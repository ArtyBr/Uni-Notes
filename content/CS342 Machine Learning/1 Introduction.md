A computer program is said to **learn** from **experience** $E$ (*data*) with respect to some class of **tasks** $T$ and **performance measure** $P$, *if* its performance at tasks in $T$, as *measured* by $P$, *improves* with **experience** $E$
- ML's performance *depends* on the *representation* of the data, i.e. on experience $E$
- ML does not need to examine the input data (e.g. images) *directly*
	- Uses several pieces of *relevant information* from images, such as the shape of eyes, mouth, complexion, etc.
	- Each piece of information is a **feature**

**Representation learning** enables ML systems to *adapt* to new tasks
- Helps to *discover* a good set of **features**

**Deep learning** *solves* representation learning:
- learn *both* the **features** *and* the **task** simultaneously
- Enables the computer to build complex concepts out of simpler concepts
