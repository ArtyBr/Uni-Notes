The **central nervous system** controls most functions of the body.
Consists of two parts:
- The **brain**
- The **spinal cord**

![[Pasted image 20260119164936.png]]

Our brain contains 100's of billions of neurons
- A **neuron** is the basic functional unit of the nervous system
- Aka **nerve cell** or **neural processing unit**
- Neurons generate **electrical signals** called **action potentials**, which allow them to transmit information over distances

4 Basic Structures of Biological Neurons:
- **Dendrite**
	- *Receives* signals from other neurons
		- Tree-like structures that extend away from the cell body to receive messages from other neurons
		- On the surface, there are many small mushroom-shapes protrusions (called **spines**) that receive excitatory input from axons
		- Once a signal is received, it travels to the **soma**
		- ![[Pasted image 20260119165424.png]]
- **Soma**
	- *Processes* the information
		- Cell body, where the nucleus lies and the neuron's DNA is housed
		- The soma takes all info from dendrites and puts them together in an area called axon hillock
		- The **Axon hillock** controls the firing of the neuron.
		- If the total strength exceeds the threshold limit of the axon hillock, the structure will **fire** a signal (aka action potential) down the axon
		- ![[Pasted image 20260119165633.png]]
- **Axon**
	- *Transmits* the information to other neurons or muscles
		- Tube-like nerve fibre that propagates the integrated signal from the cell body to specialised endings called **axon terminals**
		- The **larger the diameter** of the axon, the **faster** it transmits information
		- Some axons (but not all) are covered with a fatty substance called **myelin** that acts as an insulator to protect the axons.
		- **Myelinated** axons transmit information **faster** than other neurons
- **Synapse**
	- Point of *connection* to other neurons

---

**How do neurons work?**
- Our sense organs (eyes, ears etc.) interact with the outside world
- They relay **visual** and **audio** information to the neuron
- Neurons generate **electrical signals** called **action potentials** which allows them to transmit information over distances.
- The neuron may get activated **only when its criteria is met**, and produces a response

In reality it is **not only a single neuron which does the work**. 
- There is a massively parallel interconnected network of neurons
- The parallel network ensures that there is **division of work**
- Each neuron performs a **certain role** or responds to a certain **stimulus**
- Some neurons may fire in **response** to the information, and in turn **relay information** to other neurons they are connected to

---

**Three classes of Neurons:** (based on role)
- **Sensory Neurons**
	- *Location*: **Receptors** (eyes, ears, tongue)
	- *Role*:
		- **Tell** the brain what is **happening** outside the body, producing our 'sensations'
	- Receptor $\rightarrow$ CNS
- **Motor Neurons**
	- *Location*: **Motor Cortex & CNS** (brain and spinal cord)
	- *Role*:
		- **Allow** the brain to **control** our **muscle movements**
	- CNS $\rightarrow$ Effector
- **Relay (Inter-) Neurons**
	- *Location*: In the **CNS** (brain and spinal cord)
	- *Role*:
		- Allow **sensory** and **motor** neurons to **communicate**
	- CNS $\rightarrow$ CNS

Nerves pass information from the neurons to allow them to work collaboratively.
