The **cell membrane** is a double layer of **lipids** and **proteins** that **separates** the **contents** of the cell from the **external environment**

![[Pasted image 20260121121904.png]]

- A **membrane potential** (aka membrane voltage) is the **difference** in voltage (electric potential) when we place one electrode on the **outside** and the other on the **inside** of the membrane of the cell
- The membrane potential is caused by the **unequal distribution** of **ions** on **different sides of the membrane**

![[Pasted image 20260121122042.png]]

- When a neuron is **not** sending a signal, it is '*at rest*' (or 'not firing')
- A **resting potential** is the difference in voltage across the membrane when a neuron is **at rest**
	- In a typical resting potential, the inside of the neuron is **more negative** relative to the outside (approximately $-70\text{mV}$)

---

There are **two channels** in the membrane:
- **Leaky channel**:
	- **Always open**, allowing the **free passage** of sodium ions and potassium ions across the membrane
- **Voltage-gated channel**
	- **Only open at certain voltages**, and are often **closed** at **resting potential**

![[Pasted image 20260121122321.png]]

The reason why the **resting potential** is negative:
- There is a **higher concentration** of potassium ions inside the cell.
	- This creates a **potassium ion gradient**, enabling potassium ions to pass through **potassium leaky channels** easily
- There is a **sodium-potassium pump** that **uses energy** to move *three* sodium ions *out* of the neuron for every *two* potassium ions it puts *in*.

![[Pasted image 20260121122528.png]]

An **action potential** is the **rapid change** in voltage across the membrane that occurs when a neuron **sends information** down an axon to reach the axon terminal
- Aka *nerve impulses* or *spikes*
- An action potential is generated when a **stimulus changes** the membrane potential to the values of a **threshold** ($-55$mV)

![[Pasted image 20260121123030.png]]

The action potential behaves using the **All-or-None** principle.
- Any **subthreshold** stimulus will cause **nothing**, while **threshold** and **supratheshold** stimuli produce a **full response** of the excitable cell

![[Pasted image 20260121123210.png]]

There are **several stages** of voltage change
- **Polarisation**
	- **Outside** of the membrane is **positive** and the **inside** of the membrane is **negative**
	- ![[Pasted image 20260121123529.png]]
- **Hyperpolarisation**
	- Membrane potential becomes **more negative** than it is at **resting potential**
	- ![[Pasted image 20260121123537.png]]
- **Depolarisation**
	- Membrane potential becomes **more positive** than it is at **resting potential**

**Synapses**
A **synapse** is a junction between **one nerve cell** and another **nerve cell**
- Most synapses are **chemical**
- Their action potential is transmitted from the axon terminal to the target cell by chemical substances (called neurotransmitters)
- If a neurotransmitter **stimulates** the **target cell** into **action**, it is an **excitatory** neurotransmitter
- If it **inhibits** the target cell, it is an **inhibitory** neurotransmitter

Most common type is **axodendritic**

The **key components** of neural signal processing are:
1. **Signals** from neurons are **collected** by the **dendrites**
2. The **soma** (cell body) **aggregates** the **incoming signals**
3. When **sufficient input** is received (i.e. a threshold is exceeded), the neuron **generates** an **action potential** (fires)
4. The **action potential** is **transmitted** along the axon to other neurons, or to structures **outside** the nervous system (e.g. muscles)

![[Pasted image 20260121124710.png]]

5. If sufficient input is **not received**, the inputs quickly **decay** and **no action potential** is generated
6. **Timing** is clearly **important** - input signals must **arrive together**; **strong inputs** will **generate more action potentials** per **unit time**
